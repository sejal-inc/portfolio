The setup

A blog that fed the sales pipeline

The company sells expense management software to small and midsized businesses, mostly in the US. The blog was one of the main ways buyers found it, and the marketing team’s goal was qualified leads.

Writers owned the words. How the blog looked, read and was built was mine. I joined as an intern in July 2023, embedded in the marketing team, and went full time in May 2024.

How do you make long, dense finance content something people want to read?

Pink era · 2023 to 2024

The first system, and visuals that break up long posts

There was no blog design system when I joined, so I built the first one. While building it, I noticed the banners weren’t the problem. The posts were: long walls of text on dense topics.

I started designing infographics inside the posts, explaining a process in one picture instead of five paragraphs. They were live across the blog before the end of 2023. Two of the largest companies in the category then began breaking up their content the same way.

Purple era · 2024 to 2025

A recognisable look and easier reading

The company refreshed its brand in October 2024. I rebuilt the blog system with it, aiming for a look people would recognise before they saw the logo. Then each new feature answered one reading problem:

Progress barReaders can’t tell how much is left · Nov 2024
FAQsFollow-up questions go unanswered · Jun 2025
Table of contentsReaders can’t jump to the part they need · Jul 2025

I also designed our annual benchmark report almost end to end. It mostly reached people already on our site: well designed, and still underperformed. Who a report reaches is part of the design.

Green era · 2025 to now

From flat images to components, ready for AI search

After an acquisition, the company rebranded in October 2025. I rebuilt the system a third time, and turned the blog’s recurring visuals into reusable CMS components. Images were slow to update, broke on phones, were invisible to search engines and couldn’t be measured. Components fixed all four.

~50%blog traffic lost as search moved to AI answers
−8%only, among readers ready to buy
AI > linksAI tools overtook backlinks as a referral source

I can’t claim the components caused that. What I can show is that when search changed, the content was already in a form machines can read.

Measuring it · 2025 to 2026

A failed first attempt, then a working system

My first attempt failed because visuals were images: there was no way to tie a number to one visual. Components solved that. In 2026 I built a registry of 16 blog features, a map of every page each appears on, a clean baseline across four data sources and a nine-page dashboard.

Desktop33%
All readers28%
Mobile19%

Readers who reach halfway through a post · September 2026 baseline

That gave the reading work a hard number to move, and pointed to mobile as the next problem.

The dashboard creates the shortlist. Testing creates the evidence.

What I learned

What I’d carry into any team

Readability compoundsWork done to help people read ended up helping machines read too.
